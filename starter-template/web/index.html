How much storm runoff does each part of a catchment produce?

Problem. During Cyclone Fengal (29 November - 3 December 2024) about 127 mm of rain fell on the hills south-east of VIT. Which sub-catchments produced the most runoff, and how much water reached the stream each hour? Objective. Compute runoff depth and volume for every sub-catchment with the SCS Curve Number method, from open data, in a page anyone can use.

This page is the worked example of the CivilTech Innovation Starter. Replace the text, the data files in web/data/ and the Java classes in src/main/java/app/ with your own project. The layout, charts, map, 3D view and deployment keep working.

1. Input data

Sub-catchments (CSV)

Columns: name, centroid_lat, centroid_lon, area_km2, curve_number (other columns are ignored). Sample: five tributaries and the main-stream corridor of the demonstration catchment, derived from the Copernicus DEM and ESA WorldCover.

Hourly rainfall (CSV)

One row per hour, rainfall in mm in the last column. Sample: ERA5 reanalysis for Cyclone Fengal at the catchment centroid (96 hours).

3. Method and data

SCS Curve Number (USDA NRCS, National Engineering Handbook Part 630, Chapter 10): for cumulative rainfall P, potential retention S = 25400/CN - 254 mm and initial abstraction Ia = 0.2 S, the cumulative runoff is Q = (P - Ia)2 / (P - Ia + S) when P > Ia. Hourly runoff is the difference of consecutive cumulative values; volume = depth × area (1 mm over 1 km² = 1000 m³). The calculation runs in Java (src/main/java/app), translated to JavaScript by TeaVM.

Limitations. Runoff volumes only (no routing to the outlet, no baseflow); curve numbers assume hydrologic soil group C; ERA5 is a 0.25° reanalysis and smooths local extremes. Results are for learning, not design.
